# Research leads — Wing II figures

**Nothing on this page is a source yet.** Every line is a document somebody still has to
open. `facts[].s` means where you actually looked, and none of these were looked at.

Opened 2026-09-19, when `facts` was added to Wing II and `npm run check` began naming the
eleven lives that state a hard figure with no block to source it in. A `press-researcher`
sweep ran the same day and returned candidate sources for all fifteen figures — and
reported, at the top of its memo, that every outbound fetch in that environment was
refused by the network policy. It had worked entirely from search results quoting the
documents. Verified independently before the memo was accepted: every request to
nature.com, jamanetwork.com, sciencedirect.com and wikipedia.org failed to connect.

So the memo is kept here as leads, and the entries were left with their figures unsourced
and the warning visible. An unsourced figure a reader can see the house has not sourced is
recoverable. Eleven citations to papers nobody opened would not be.

**To close this out:** open the documents below in an environment that can fetch, confirm
the wording, and write `facts` blocks from what you read. Delete each row as it lands.

## A new lead, opened 2026-09-20

**What the 1935 Social Security Act left out.** `frances-perkins` says in `contested` that
whole categories of work were excluded from the original coverage and that historians
disagree about why — whether it was the price of passage through a Congress whose
committees Southern Democrats chaired, or something closer to a choice. That sentence
names no scholar on purpose. The two standard positions are well known, but neither
document was opened for this entry: ssa.gov returns 403 to robots from here, and the
NPS and Roosevelt Library pages that were opened do not touch the exclusions at all.

Open one from each side, then either name them in `contested` or say plainly that the
house has not read them. Until then the entry claims a disagreement exists and does not
claim to have adjudicated it, which is the honest shape.

## Two that are decisions, not lookups

**Borlaug's billion. Decided 2026-09-21: cut (correction 11).** The sweep traced the figure to Gregg Easterbrook, "Forgotten
Benefactor of Humanity", *The Atlantic*, January 1997 — and found that Easterbrook's own
sentence is hedged: the agriculture Borlaug preached *may have* prevented a billion
deaths. No published methodology was found behind it, then or since: no counterfactual
model, no demographic calculation, nothing peer-reviewed. What exists is the number
repeated by the World Food Prize Foundation, the obituaries and the advocacy press, with
some 2009 retrospectives using the more conservative "hundreds of millions" instead.

That makes it unsourceable in the sense the house means. A `facts` line naming Easterbrook
would be accurate about provenance and dishonest about status: it would print a
journalist's hedge as a demographic finding.

It is also an inconsistency the house can settle without opening anything. Tu Youyou's
entry refused this exact class of number — *"no 'lives saved' figure: none exists for
artemisinin alone, and the house will not borrow the all-interventions number"* — while
Borlaug's prints one. Two entries, one shelf, opposite rules.

The choice is Luca's, and either way it is `npm run correct` and not a quiet edit:
attribute it in the prose as after Easterbrook and keep it out of `facts`, or follow the
Tu Youyou precedent and cut the number, leaving `contested` to say that the scale is
sharply argued.

**The Jobs paternity figure.** The entry prints 94.1 per cent. *Time*'s January 1983
profile quotes the test report at 94.1; Lisa Brennan-Jobs' own memoir, *Small Fry* (2018),
gives 94.4. Two primary-adjacent sources, one number apart, and the second is the person
the test was about. Nothing found resolves it. Whatever is printed, the other figure
belongs in the entry or in a correction — the house does not get to pick the tidier one
silently.
