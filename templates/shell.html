Colophon

This is a library, not a credential. It holds what I have read, what I think it means, and — deliberately, in every wing — the places where the people who know most still disagree. A claim without its dispute is half a claim.

The three wings are one method applied to people, three ways round. The Press is the ideas people spent their lives inside; Lives is the people themselves, one book each; The Atlas is the people I listen to now, and the principles more than one of them arrived at independently. The library used to run to five wings — a set of field manuals and a trade book of operating lessons. Both were positions stated without anyone standing behind them, and they came down in September 2026 so that everything here is attached to a person you can go and check.

Set in EB Garamond and IBM Plex Mono. Assembled by hand and with AI assistance; figures are checked against a named source, and where I have corrected myself the correction stays visible rather than being quietly patched. The reading wings are set on paper. The Atlas is the only room kept dark, because a star chart is the one thing that genuinely needs a night sky.

The portraits in Lives are public-domain engravings, paintings and photographs, reduced to the house duotone and set as plates. A life without a safely public image carries the press mark instead of a borrowed picture, which seems the more honest trade. {{W_PLATELESS_CAP}} lives are in that position today. Henrietta Lacks is there by decision; her entry says why at length, and her estate has itself disputed the use of family photographs. Ibn Khaldun, Clair Patterson and Vasily Arkhipov are there because no image of any of them has yet been cleared: no portrait of Ibn Khaldun from his own lifetime is known, Patterson's photographs sit in an archive that grants permission only on request, and the one openly licensed photograph of Arkhipov was tagged by an uploader who does not appear to have held the rights to grant it. {{W_CCPLATES_CAP}} plates are licensed rather than public domain, and are credited here because the licence requires it: the Mandela photograph is by South Africa The Good News (sagoodnews.co.za) under CC BY 2.0; the Steve Jobs photograph was taken at WWDC in 2010 by Matthew Yohe under CC BY-SA 3.0; the Tu Youyou photograph was taken in Stockholm on 6 December 2015 by Bengt Nyman under CC BY-SA 4.0; and the 1955 photograph of Rosalind Franklin at her microscope is credited to the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology, from the personal collection of her sister Jenifer Glynn, under CC BY-SA 4.0. Each has been cropped and reduced to the house duotone, which the licences ask us to say. Three more are neither plain public domain nor licensed but held under a narrower claim, and the claim is worth repeating in the holder's own words: the 1939 photograph of Robert Moses is by C. M. Stieglitz for the New York World-Telegram & Sun, held by the Library of Congress, which states that it knows of no copyright restrictions on it; the 1963 photograph of Wu Chien-Shiung at Columbia comes from the Smithsonian Institution Archives, published with no known copyright restrictions; and the photograph of Frances Kelsey receiving her award from President Kennedy comes from the National Library of Medicine, which believes the item to be in the public domain. Everything else came through Wikimedia Commons.

Wing III · the sources · kept dark

Debts & Lessons

A constellation is a principle more than one source handed me independently, across totally different lives — a lion tracker, a surgeon, a fintech founder arriving at the same line. The repetitions are the part I trust. These are sources I listened to, not verdicts on the people — and the wing takes its name from Marcus Aurelius, whose Meditations open with a book of exactly this: debts and lessons, with names attached.
